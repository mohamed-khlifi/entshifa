       documents it must produce, and the order to build it in.

            (React / Next.js / shadcn front end, FastAPI back end, MySQL).

1. WHAT CHANGED COMPARED TO THE FIRST DRAFT, AND WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first draft was a reasonable skeleton. It had the right instinct
(structured data first, AI last), but it described the application from a
software engineer's point of view. It listed database tables and API routes
and said "add validated scores later." An ENT doctor reading it would not
recognize their own working day in it.

This version starts from what ENT doctors actually do in a week:

  - They see a high volume of short consultations dominated by a small set
    of complaints (nasal obstruction, ear pain, hearing loss, sore throat,
    vertigo, hoarseness, snoring, neck lump). Speed matters more than
    completeness on most visits.
  - They perform office procedures during the visit: nasal endoscopy,
    fiberoptic laryngoscopy, otomicroscopy, wax removal, epistaxis cautery,
    Epley maneuvers, tube insertion. These need a report, often on the spot.
  - They read tests: audiograms, tympanograms, OAE, ABR, vestibular tests,
    sinus CT, neck ultrasound, sleep studies, allergy panels. The results
    need to be stored as numbers, not as scanned PDFs.
  - They score things constantly: SNOT-22, NOSE, VHI, THI, DHI, Epworth,
    STOP-BANG, Brodsky, Lund-Mackay, Lund-Kennedy, House-Brackmann, RSI,
    EAT-10, TNM. Today these are done on paper or in the doctor's head.
  - They operate. The consultation is often a step towards surgery, and
    surgery generates its own paperwork (consent, quote, pre-op checklist,
    operative note, post-op plan).
  - They write a lot of documents: consultation report, procedure report,
    prescription, referral letter, sick leave, medical certificate, imaging
    request, lab request, patient instructions.
  - They follow patients over years: cholesteatoma surveillance, tube
    checks, yearly audiograms, post-cancer surveillance schedules,
    immunotherapy schedules.

What is new in this version:

  - The consultation is organized around the chief complaint. Choosing the
    complaint configures the whole visit (history fields, exam sections,
    tests to expect, questionnaires to send, likely documents).
  - Interactive anatomical maps (tympanic membrane, nasal cavity, larynx,
    oral cavity, neck lymph node levels, face) replace long dropdown lists.
  - Full audiology suite instead of "audiogram calculator": tympanometry,
    speech audiometry, OAE, ABR, hearing aid tracking, WHO grading,
    asymmetry and threshold-shift alerts, overlay comparison.
  - Full vestibular suite with canal-specific BPPV logic and maneuver
    documentation.
  - Seven sub-specialty modules that were missing entirely (tinnitus,
    voice and swallowing, sleep, allergy, head and neck oncology,
    pediatrics, facial nerve, salivary, epistaxis).
  - A questionnaire engine where patients fill scores on a tablet or via
    QR code before the visit, so results are waiting for the doctor.
  - A surgery module, because surgery is half of ENT.
  - A documents module that covers everything the doctor prints, not only
    the "report".
  - A follow-up (recall) engine that knows the standard surveillance
    intervals for ENT conditions and procedures.
  - Safety alerts specific to ENT: only-hearing-ear, ototoxic drugs,
    anticoagulants before procedures, drug allergies on prescriptions.
  - A doctor dashboard with clinical outcomes (for example mean SNOT-22
    improvement after sinus surgery in that doctor's own patients).
  - Clear multilingual design (French, Arabic, English) with right-to-left
    support for Arabic patient documents.

The AI section is still the smallest. That was the right call in the first
draft and it stays.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PRODUCT VISION AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One sentence:

  An ENT doctor should finish a consultation, its procedure report, the
  prescription and the follow-up plan before the patient has put their
  coat back on, and everything should be stored as data they can compare
  next year.

Design principles (these decide arguments later):

 P1. The chief complaint drives everything. Nobody should scroll past
     vestibular fields when the patient came for a nosebleed.

 P2. Every finding is a structured value first and a sentence second. The
     sentence is generated from the value. The doctor edits the sentence
     if needed, but the value is what gets stored and compared.

 P3. Normal is one click. "Normal otoscopy, both ears" is a single action
     that fills every sub-field and writes the negative findings into the
     report.

 P4. Copy forward, then show the difference. Every visit can start from
     the previous one. The app highlights what changed.

 P5. Numbers stay numbers. Audiogram thresholds, wheal sizes, AHI, SNOT-22
     totals are stored as numeric fields so they can be graphed and
     compared. Never as text inside a PDF.

 P6. The app calculates, the doctor interprets. The app says "interaural
     asymmetry of 25 dB at 4 kHz meets the configured asymmetry criterion."
     It does not say "suspect acoustic neuroma."

 P7. Anything printed comes from a template the doctor controls.

 P8. Keyboard first, tablet second, mouse third. Doctors in a procedure
     room have one hand on an endoscope.

 P9. Nothing is lost. Autosave every few seconds, drafts survive a browser
     crash, every clinical change is audited.

 P10. AI is optional, visible, and never writes into the record without a
      human confirming.

3. A DAY IN AN ENT PRACTICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The application must support these five settings. Each has different
constraints.

3.1 Reception and waiting room
  - Patient is registered or found (name, phone, national ID, insurance).
  - Reason for visit is captured in the patient's words.
  - Patient receives a tablet or a QR code and fills the questionnaire
    matching the complaint (SNOT-22 for sinus, THI for tinnitus, Epworth
    for snoring, DHI for vertigo, VHI-10 for voice). Score is ready when
    the doctor opens the chart.
  - Assistant records weight and height (essential for pediatric dosing),
    temperature if febrile, and any new allergy.
  - Assistant or technician may perform screening audiometry and
    tympanometry and enter results directly. The doctor sees them already
    plotted.

3.2 Consultation room
  - Doctor opens the patient. Sees a one-screen summary: alerts, active
    problems, last visit summary, pending results, today's questionnaire
    score.
  - Selects or confirms the chief complaint. The consultation cockpit
    reconfigures.
  - Takes history with structured fields plus a free-text box.
  - Examines using anatomical maps and one-click normals.
  - Decides. Records diagnosis (coded), plan, prescriptions, requests,
    follow-up.
  - Prints or sends documents. All in under 10 minutes for a routine
    visit.

3.3 Procedure room (office procedures)
  - Nasal endoscopy, fiberoptic laryngoscopy, videostroboscopy,
    otomicroscopy with wax or foreign body removal, epistaxis cautery,
    tympanostomy under local, Epley or other repositioning maneuver,
    biopsy, botulinum injection, in-office FNA.
  - Doctor has one free hand at most. The interface must work with
    keyboard shortcuts, foot pedal image capture (later), or an assistant
    entering findings as the doctor dictates.
  - Images and short video clips are captured and attached to findings.
  - Report is generated immediately and signed.

3.4 Operating day
  - Surgeon reviews the day's list with pre-op checklist status.
  - After surgery, writes the operative note from a procedure-specific
    template (structured fields, not a blank page).
  - Post-op plan and follow-up appointments are generated automatically
    from the procedure type.
  - Procedure is added to the surgeon's logbook.

3.5 Follow-up and administrative time
  - Signing reports that were left as drafts.
  - Reviewing results that arrived (audiograms from external lab, CT
    reports, pathology).
  - Handling recalls: who is overdue for tube check, cholesteatoma
    review, cancer surveillance, immunotherapy dose.
  - Answering referral letters.

Pain points doctors report about existing generic software, which this
product must fix:

  - Fields designed for general medicine. No place to record a tympanic
    membrane perforation location, a polyp grade, or vocal cord mobility.
  - Audiograms stored as scanned images. Impossible to compare visits.
  - Reports rewritten by hand every time. The same "Normal nasal
    endoscopy" text typed 20 times a day.
  - Scores calculated on paper and never stored.
  - No memory of follow-up rules. Patients with tubes fall through.
  - Surgery paperwork done in Word documents outside the system.
  - Prescriptions without pediatric weight-based dosing help.
  - Nothing in Arabic for the patient, nothing in French for the record.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FOUNDATION MODULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Patient registry
  Identity
    - Full name, date of birth (age auto-computed, shown in years for
      adults and in months for children under 3), sex, national ID or
      passport, phone (primary and secondary), email, address, preferred
      language for documents (French / Arabic / English).
    - Insurance: scheme (national health insurance, private, none),
      member number, coverage notes. Keep it a configurable list because
      it varies by country.
    - Referring doctor (name, specialty, phone, email) so referral replies
      can be generated.
    - Emergency contact. For minors, parent or guardian name and
      relationship.
    - Occupation and noise exposure (relevant for hearing loss). Smoking
      and alcohol (relevant for head and neck cancer and voice).
  Duplicate detection at creation: same name plus date of birth or same
  phone triggers a "possible existing patient" prompt.
  Merge patients (admin only, audited).

4.2 Clinical background (persistent across visits)
  - Drug allergies with reaction type and severity.
  - Other allergies (latex, iodine contrast, food).
  - Chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, asthma, GERD, coagulation
    disorders, immunosuppression).
  - Current medications with a flag for anticoagulants / antiplatelets
    and for known ototoxic drugs.
  - Past ENT surgeries (procedure, side, year, surgeon, complications).
  - Other surgeries.
  - Hearing aids or cochlear implant (see audiology module).
  - Family history relevant to ENT (hearing loss, thyroid disease, head
    and neck cancer, otosclerosis).
  - Problem list: active and resolved diagnoses, each coded.
  - ENT-specific persistent flags (see 4.5 alerts).

4.3 Clinic, staff and roles
  - Clinic profile: name, address, phone, logo, header and footer text for
    documents, default language, tax or registration numbers for quotes.
  - Multiple sites allowed under one clinic (a doctor working in a private
    office and a hospital).
  - Roles:
      Clinic admin      manages users, templates, billing settings, merges
      Doctor            full clinical access to their patients, signs
                        documents
      Assistant / nurse registers patients, enters vitals, questionnaires,
                        schedules, prints unsigned documents, cannot sign
      Audiology technician  enters audiometry, tympanometry, OAE, ABR;
                        cannot see consultation notes beyond what is needed
      Read-only         for a locum or a student, with audit
  - A doctor sees their own patients by default and other doctors'
    patients in the same clinic only if the admin enables shared access.

4.4 Schedule (light version, deliberately minimal)
  - Day and week view of appointments per doctor and per room.
  - Appointment types with default durations: new consultation, follow-up,
    procedure (endoscopy, laryngoscopy, microscopy), audiology test slot,
    post-op check, surgery.
  - Status: scheduled, waiting, in room, done, no-show.
  - Waiting-room list with arrival time and questionnaire status
    ("SNOT-22 completed").
  - Book the follow-up directly from the consultation plan ("see in 6
    weeks with audiogram" creates the appointment and the test slot).
  This is not a full practice-management calendar. No online booking, no
  recurring rules, no resource optimization in version 1.

4.5 Safety alerts (banner at the top of the patient screen)
  These are the ENT-specific alerts that generic software never has.
  - ONLY HEARING EAR: patient has a dead or near-dead ear on one side.
    Shown in red on every screen. Any procedure or ototoxic prescription
    on the good side asks for explicit confirmation.
  - Anticoagulant / antiplatelet in current medications. Shown before any
    procedure (biopsy, cautery, surgery) with the drug name.
  - Drug allergy. Shown on the prescription screen and blocks prescribing
    the allergen class without an override reason.
  - Ototoxic medication present (aminoglycosides, cisplatin or
    carboplatin, loop diuretics, high-dose salicylates, quinine). Prompts
    baseline and monitoring audiometry.
  - Immunosuppressed or diabetic when otitis externa or invasive fungal
    sinusitis is being documented.
  - Pediatric weight missing when a prescription is started for a child.
  - Airway alert: history of difficult airway, tracheostomy, laryngeal
    stenosis.
  - Pacemaker or cochlear implant (relevant for monopolar cautery and MRI
    requests).
  - Pregnancy or breastfeeding (prescriptions and imaging).
  Alerts are structured flags with a start date, source and optional end
  date. They are not free text.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONSULTATION COCKPIT (THE MAIN SCREEN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Layout
  Left column (fixed): patient card with alerts, age, allergies, active
    problems, current medications, last three visits in one line each,
    today's questionnaire score.
  Center: the consultation itself in collapsible sections that follow the
    clinical order: reason, history, examination, tests, assessment, plan.
  Right column: live report preview. As the doctor fills fields, the
    sentences appear. Editable in place. This is what will be printed.
  Bottom bar: autosave status, "normal exam" shortcuts, sign, print,
    "copy from last visit".

5.2 Chief complaint selector
  A grid of the common ENT complaints, grouped by region, plus search.
    Ear:    ear pain, hearing loss, ear discharge, tinnitus, ear fullness,
            itching, foreign body, wax, sudden hearing loss
    Nose:   nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, sneezing / itching, epistaxis,
            loss of smell, facial pain or pressure, post-nasal drip,
            nasal deformity, nasal trauma
    Throat: sore throat, recurrent tonsillitis, dysphagia, globus, reflux
            symptoms, bad breath, oral lesion, dental-related pain
    Voice / airway: hoarseness, voice fatigue, stridor, chronic cough,
            breathing difficulty
    Neck:   neck lump, thyroid nodule, salivary swelling, neck pain
    Sleep:  snoring, witnessed apneas, daytime sleepiness, child mouth
            breathing
    Balance: vertigo, dizziness, imbalance, motion intolerance
    Face:   facial weakness, facial pain, facial swelling
    Pediatric: recurrent ear infections, speech delay, failed hearing
            screen, noisy breathing
    Follow-up: post-operative check, results review, chronic disease
            review, tube check, immunotherapy visit
  More than one complaint can be selected. The first one is primary.

5.3 What the complaint changes (the core innovation of this screen)
  Selecting a complaint applies a "visit template" that:
    a. Adds the complaint-specific history questions (see 5.4).
    b. Expands the relevant examination sections and collapses the others
       (they remain available one click away).
    c. Pre-loads the matching questionnaire result if the patient filled
       it in the waiting room, or offers to send it.
    d. Suggests the usual tests for that complaint as tick boxes (for
       nasal obstruction: nasal endoscopy today, sinus CT request; for
       hearing loss: audiogram, tympanogram).
    e. Pre-selects the likely documents (for recurrent tonsillitis in a
       child: tonsillectomy information sheet, surgery quote).
    f. Loads the doctor's own favorite diagnoses and prescriptions for
       this complaint.
  Templates ship with sensible defaults and every doctor can edit their
  own copy. Appendix B lists the default mapping.

5.4 History (structured plus free text)
  Common to all complaints:
    onset (date or duration), course (constant, intermittent, progressive,
    improving), laterality (right, left, bilateral, alternating), severity
    (0 to 10 or mild / moderate / severe), triggers, relieving factors,
    previous treatments tried and response, previous consultations for the
    same problem, impact on work / school / sleep.
  Complaint-specific blocks, examples:
    Nasal obstruction: constant vs alternating, worse lying down, mouth
      breathing at night, snoring, hyposmia, facial pressure, discharge
      character, allergic symptoms and seasonality, nasal spray overuse
      (decongestant rhinitis), previous nasal surgery or trauma.
    Hearing loss: sudden vs progressive, fluctuating, with tinnitus or
      vertigo, noise exposure, ototoxic drugs, family history, ear
      infections in childhood, hearing aid use, difficulty in noise vs
      quiet, which ear is used for the phone.
    Vertigo: see vestibular suite (section 9), the history is the
      diagnosis in vertigo.
    Hoarseness: duration, voice use (teacher, singer, call center),
      smoking, reflux, intubation history, breathing or swallowing
      symptoms, weight loss (red flag), hemoptysis (red flag).
    Sore throat / tonsillitis: number of episodes in the last 12 months
      and in each of the previous 2 years (feeds the tonsillectomy
      criteria, see calculator engine), antibiotic courses, missed school
      or work days, abscess history, snoring.
    Neck lump: duration, growth rate, pain, associated fever, weight
      loss, night sweats, dysphagia, voice change, smoking and alcohol,
      dental problems, recent infection, animal contact, tuberculosis
      exposure.
    Epistaxis: side, frequency, duration, anterior vs posterior pattern,
      anticoagulants, hypertension, nose picking, dry environment,
      bleeding elsewhere, family history of bleeding.
    Snoring / sleep: Epworth and STOP-BANG scores, witnessed apneas,
      nocturia, morning headaches, weight change, bed partner report,
      previous sleep study (AHI), CPAP use and tolerance.
  Free-text box always present. Anything the structured fields do not
  capture goes there, and the AI assistant can later propose structured
  values from it (section 18).

5.5 Red flag capture
  Certain answers set a visible red-flag marker on the consultation and
  in the report (sudden sensorineural hearing loss, unilateral tinnitus
  with asymmetric hearing, hoarseness over 3 weeks in a smoker, neck lump
  over 3 weeks in an adult, unilateral nasal obstruction with bleeding,
  stridor, dysphagia with weight loss, facial weakness with a parotid
  mass). The app does not diagnose. It marks and lets the doctor decide.
  The list of red flags is editable by the clinic.

5.6 Assessment and plan
  - Diagnoses: search by name or code, the doctor's favorites first,
    complaint-specific favorites above that. Each diagnosis has side
    (right / left / bilateral / not applicable), status (suspected,
    confirmed, ruled out), and can be promoted to the problem list.
  - Plan items, each structured: medication (opens prescription),
    procedure done today, test requested, imaging requested, referral,
    surgery proposed (opens surgery module), advice given (from a library
    of handouts), follow-up (opens recall engine), sick leave, certificate.
  - Every plan item becomes a sentence in the report and, where relevant,
    a document.

5.7 One-click normals and copy forward
  - "Normal ENT examination" fills all exam sections with normal values
    and generates the negative-findings text. The doctor then changes
    only what is abnormal.
  - Per-region normals: "Normal otoscopy R", "Normal otoscopy L", "Normal
    anterior rhinoscopy", "Normal oral cavity and oropharynx", "Normal
    neck".
  - "Copy from last visit" pre-fills history, exam and problem list from
    the previous consultation and shows each copied value in a distinct
    style until the doctor confirms or changes it. The report then states
    "unchanged compared to [date]" where the doctor wants it.

5.8 Signing and locking
  - A consultation is a draft until signed. Signing locks it. After
    signing, corrections are added as dated addenda, never as silent
    edits. The original stays visible in the audit trail.
  - Unsigned drafts appear on the doctor's dashboard so nothing stays
    unsigned for days.

6. EXAMINATION MODULE WITH INTERACTIVE ANATOMICAL MAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The examination is where dropdown-heavy medical software fails ENT. The
answer is clickable schematic diagrams with structured findings attached to
anatomical positions. Each map has a right and left version where the
organ is paired.

6.1 Ear: tympanic membrane map (otoscopy / otomicroscopy)
  The diagram shows the external canal and a tympanic membrane divided
  into the four quadrants (anterosuperior, anteroinferior, posterosuperior,
  posteroinferior) plus pars flaccida, umbo, handle of malleus, light
  reflex.
  External canal findings: normal, wax (partial / complete occlusion),
    otitis externa (edema, debris, fungal elements), exostoses, foreign
    body, stenosis, granulation, discharge (serous, mucoid, purulent,
    bloody, foul-smelling), skin lesion.
  Membrane findings, placed by clicking the quadrant:
    perforation (central / marginal / attic, quadrant(s) involved,
      estimated percentage of surface, dry or wet, edges visible or not,
      middle ear mucosa visible: normal / inflamed / polypoid)
    retraction (pars tensa graded with Sadé, pars flaccida graded with
      Tos, self-cleaning or not, keratin debris)
    cholesteatoma (location, extent, active discharge)
    tympanosclerosis (plaques, location)
    effusion (serous / mucoid / air-fluid level / bubbles, membrane color:
      amber, blue, dull)
    acute otitis media (bulging, erythema, purulent effusion, ruptured)
    hemotympanum
    ventilation tube (type, in place and patent / blocked / extruded /
      lying in canal, granulation around it, otorrhea through it)
    bullae, granulation, polyp, mass
    myringitis
  Mobility: normal, reduced, absent, hypermobile (pneumatic otoscopy).
  Tuning fork tests: Weber (midline / lateralizes right / left), Rinne
    right and left (positive / negative), stored as structured values
    with the frequency used (256 or 512 Hz).
  Facial nerve function is recorded on the face map (6.6), not here.
  Output sentence example (generated):
    "Right ear: dry central perforation involving the anteroinferior and
     posteroinferior quadrants, approximately 40% of the membrane, middle
     ear mucosa normal. Left ear: normal."

6.2 Nose: anterior rhinoscopy map
  Diagram of the nasal cavity in coronal view, right and left.
  Structures: vestibule, septum (deviation side, location: caudal,
    anterior, posterior, spur, perforation with size), inferior turbinate
    (normal, hypertrophic, response to decongestant), middle turbinate,
    nasal valve (collapse on inspiration), mucosa (normal, pale, congested,
    edematous, atrophic, crusting), discharge (none, clear, mucopurulent,
    bloody, unilateral, posterior), polyps (grade with the chosen scale),
    masses, bleeding point (Kiesselbach area or other), synechiae, signs
    of previous surgery.
  External nose: deformity (dorsal hump, deviation, saddle, tip), skin
    lesions, swelling, trauma signs.
  Detailed nasal endoscopy has its own report builder (section 7.1) but
  the basic map is enough for a simple visit.

6.3 Oral cavity and oropharynx map
  Diagram: lips, buccal mucosa, teeth and gums, hard and soft palate,
  uvula, tonsils, posterior pharyngeal wall, tongue (dorsum, lateral
  borders, ventral), floor of mouth, salivary duct openings.
  Tonsils: Brodsky grade 0 to 4 per side, crypts with debris, exudate,
    erythema, asymmetry (a red flag when significant), peritonsillar
    swelling, tonsillolith.
  Palate: Friedman tongue position I to IV, Mallampati I to IV (useful
    for the anesthetist), long uvula, bifid uvula, cleft or submucous
    cleft.
  Mucosal lesions placed by click: ulcer, leukoplakia, erythroplakia,
    lichenoid change, mass, candidiasis, aphthous lesions, with size in
    mm and whether it is indurated. Photo attachment strongly encouraged
    for any lesion.
  Dentition: poor, missing teeth, dental abscess, prosthesis.
  Tongue mobility and deviation. Gag reflex.

6.4 Larynx and hypopharynx (mirror or endoscopic)
  Handled by the laryngoscopy report builder (7.2). The consultation
  exam offers a shortcut: "indirect laryngoscopy: normal / not possible /
  see laryngoscopy report".

6.5 Neck map with lymph node levels
  Diagram of the neck showing levels IA, IB, IIA, IIB, III, IV, VA, VB,
  VI, VII, plus thyroid lobes and isthmus, parotid, submandibular gland,
  midline.
  Clicking a level opens a node entry: size in mm (largest dimension),
    number (single / multiple / matted), consistency (soft, firm, hard,
    rubbery, fluctuant), mobility (mobile, fixed to skin, fixed to deep
    structures), tenderness, skin change.
  Thyroid: size (normal, diffusely enlarged with grade), nodule (side,
    size, consistency), tenderness, movement on swallowing, retrosternal
    extension suspected, bruit.
  Salivary glands: swelling, tenderness, stone palpable, duct discharge
    on massage, mass.
  Midline: thyroglossal cyst movement with tongue protrusion, dermoid.
  Other: trachea position, laryngeal crepitus, carotid pulsation, skin
    scars, sinus or fistula.
  Every neck mass entry can be linked to an ultrasound or FNA result
  later (head and neck module).

6.6 Face and cranial nerves map
  Facial nerve: House-Brackmann grade I to VI as a whole, with optional
    Sunnybrook composite score (resting symmetry, voluntary movement,
    synkinesis). Side. Onset date. Bell's phenomenon, eye closure
    completeness, lagophthalmos in mm.
  Facial sensation by trigeminal division (V1, V2, V3), normal or reduced.
  Facial swelling or tenderness over sinuses (frontal, maxillary,
    ethmoid), orbital signs (proptosis, chemosis, diplopia, reduced
    acuity) for sinus complications.
  Other cranial nerves relevant to skull base and head and neck: IX, X,
    XI (shoulder shrug), XII (tongue deviation).

6.7 General examination fields (compact)
  Temperature, weight, height, BMI auto-computed, blood pressure (epistaxis
  and pre-op), oxygen saturation (airway), respiratory rate and stridor
  (inspiratory / expiratory / biphasic), voice quality on entry
  (normal, hoarse, breathy, muffled "hot potato", nasal).

6.8 Behavior of all maps
  - Click a structure once: normal. Click again: opens finding picker.
  - Long press or right click: attach photo to that structure.
  - Right and left toggled with one key.
  - Everything placed on the map appears in the report preview
    immediately, in anatomical order, right before left.
  - A "not examined" state exists for every structure and produces no
    sentence (or "not examined" if the doctor wants that stated).
  - Maps can be exported as annotated images into the report.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. PROCEDURE REPORT BUILDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each office procedure has a dedicated builder that produces a signed
report in under two minutes. All builders share the same skeleton:
indication, anesthesia (none / topical spray / local infiltration),
instrument used, tolerance, findings by structure, images, conclusion,
plan. The findings part is procedure-specific.

7.1 Nasal endoscopy report builder
  Structures per side, in the order the scope passes them:
    vestibule and nasal valve, septum, inferior turbinate, inferior
    meatus, middle turbinate (concha bullosa, paradoxical), middle meatus
    and uncinate, ethmoid bulla, accessory ostia, superior turbinate and
    superior meatus, sphenoethmoidal recess, olfactory cleft, nasopharynx
    (adenoid tissue, Eustachian tube orifice, fossa of Rosenmuller,
    masses), post-operative cavity when previous FESS.
  Per structure: normal / abnormal / not visualized, then findings from
    the ENT-specific list: edema (0 to 2), polyp (0 to 2 for the
    Lund-Kennedy item, plus overall polyp grade using Meltzer 0 to 4 or
    Lildholdt 0 to 3, chosen in settings), discharge (none / clear /
    thick / purulent), crusting (0 to 2), scarring or synechiae (0 to 2),
    bleeding, mass (with description: smooth, ulcerated, friable,
    pulsatile), fungal debris, mucus recirculation, patent or blocked
    ostium, accessory ostium, exposed bone, foreign body.
  Automatic scores:
    - Lund-Kennedy endoscopic score (polyps, edema, discharge, scarring,
      crusting; 0 to 2 each per side; 0 to 20).
    - Modified Lund-Kennedy (polyps, edema, discharge only; 0 to 12).
    - Polyp grade per side.
    - Adenoid size as percentage of choanal obstruction in children
      (or a 4-grade scale, configurable).
  Images: capture or upload, attach to a structure, draw arrows or
    circles, label. Side-by-side with the previous endoscopy of the same
    structure when one exists.
  Comparison view: this endoscopy against the previous one, structure by
    structure, with the score trend.
  Report: findings right then left, then nasopharynx, then scores, then
    conclusion (doctor's free text with the AI draft offered), then plan.
  Special templates: post-FESS cavity check (patency of maxillary
    antrostomy, frontal recess, sphenoid ostium, crusting, synechiae,
    recurrent polyps), epistaxis endoscopy (bleeding site localization),
    CSF leak assessment.

7.2 Fiberoptic laryngoscopy and videostroboscopy report builder
  Structures in passage order: nasal cavity (brief), nasopharynx, velum
    (closure pattern), base of tongue and vallecula, epiglottis (shape,
    omega, edema), aryepiglottic folds, arytenoids (edema, erythema,
    mobility), pyriform sinuses, post-cricoid area (pooling), false
    cords, ventricles, true vocal folds, anterior commissure, posterior
    commissure (interarytenoid pachydermia), subglottis, upper trachea
    if visible.
  Vocal fold section (the heart of this builder):
    - Mobility right and left: normal, hypomobile, immobile (fixed or
      paralyzed), paradoxical. Position of an immobile fold: median,
      paramedian, intermediate, lateral.
    - Glottic closure: complete, anterior gap, posterior gap, spindle,
      hourglass, irregular, incomplete.
    - Lesions placed on a vocal fold diagram divided into anterior,
      middle and posterior thirds and free edge / superior surface:
      nodule, polyp (sessile / pedunculated), cyst, Reinke's edema (grade
      1 to 3), granuloma, papilloma, leukoplakia, erythroplakia,
      ulceration, mass (with size estimate), sulcus, scar, web, varix,
      hemorrhage.
    - Supraglottic activity (muscle tension pattern types).
    - Secretions, pooling, laryngopharyngeal reflux signs.
  Stroboscopy fields when available: symmetry, periodicity, amplitude
    (right / left), mucosal wave (right / left: normal / reduced /
    absent), phase closure, phase symmetry, vertical level, non-vibrating
    segment.
  Automatic scores:
    - Reflux Finding Score (8 items, 0 to 26).
    - GRBAS from the perceptual voice assessment (0 to 3 each).
    - Penetration-Aspiration Scale if FEES (functional endoscopic
      evaluation of swallowing) fields are used, with consistencies
      tested and residue location.
  Video clips (5 to 15 seconds) attached with timestamp markers on
    key frames.
  Special templates: pre-thyroidectomy vocal fold check, post-thyroidectomy
    check, vocal fold paralysis workup, FEES swallowing study, pediatric
    airway (laryngomalacia type, subglottic stenosis Myer-Cotton grade),
    tracheostomy stoma and trachea check.

7.3 Otomicroscopy and ear procedures
  Uses the tympanic membrane map (6.1) at higher detail plus a procedure
  block:
    - Wax removal (method: microsuction / curette / irrigation; complete
      or partial; canal condition after).
    - Foreign body removal (object, method, canal trauma).
    - Otitis externa toilet and wick placement (wick type, removal date
      creates a recall).
    - Myringotomy and ventilation tube insertion under local (tube type
      and side, effusion character, complications). Creates a tube-
      tracking record with automatic 6-month recall (configurable).
    - Tube removal.
    - Ear canal biopsy.
    - Intratympanic injection (drug, dose, side, indication).
    - Cholesteatoma cavity cleaning (mastoid cavity condition: dry,
      moist, granulation, debris, exposed structures) with next cleaning
      recall.
  Every ear with a ventilation tube, a cholesteatoma or a mastoid cavity
  gets a persistent tracking record visible on the patient card.

7.4 Other office procedures (short structured forms)
  - Epistaxis management: site found (yes / no, location), method
    (silver nitrate cautery, electrocautery, anterior pack with type,
    posterior pack, balloon), pack removal date (recall), blood pressure
    at the time, anticoagulant status, advice given.
  - Canalith repositioning maneuvers: see vestibular suite (9.5).
  - Fine needle aspiration in office: site, needle gauge, passes,
    ultrasound guided or not, sample sent to which lab, expected result
    date (recall for result review).
  - Oral or pharyngeal biopsy.
  - Botulinum toxin injection (spasmodic dysphonia, sialorrhea,
    cricopharyngeal): muscle, dose, side.
  - Peritonsillar abscess drainage: aspiration vs incision, volume,
    culture sent.
  - Nasal fracture reduction: closed reduction under local, splint type,
    removal date.
  - Sialendoscopy: gland, findings (stone, stenosis, mucus plug), stone
    removed or not.
  Each produces a short report and, when relevant, a recall.

8. AUDIOLOGY SUITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the module where storing numbers instead of PDFs pays off most.
It is designed so that a technician enters the data and the doctor reads
a finished dashboard.

8.1 Pure tone audiogram entry
  - Frequencies: 125, 250, 500, 750, 1000, 1500, 2000, 3000, 4000, 6000,
    8000 Hz. Inter-octave frequencies optional per clinic setting.
    Extended high frequencies (10, 12.5, 14, 16 kHz) optional for
    ototoxicity monitoring.
  - Air conduction right and left, bone conduction right and left, each
    with masked / unmasked marker and "no response at limit" marker
    (stored as the limit value plus a flag, drawn with the standard
    arrow).
  - Entry methods: type in a grid with arrow-key navigation, or click
    directly on the audiogram chart. Both update each other.
  - Standard symbols and colors (right red circles, left blue crosses,
    bone brackets) so the printed audiogram looks like every audiogram
    the doctor has ever read.
  - Transducer (headphones, inserts, free field), tester, equipment
    identifier, calibration date, reliability (good / fair / poor),
    method for children (play audiometry, VRA, BOA).
  - Import from audiometer export files where the manufacturer provides
    a format (later phase). Manual entry is the baseline.

8.2 Speech audiometry
  - Speech reception threshold per ear (dB HL), word recognition score
    at one or more presentation levels (percentage and level), word list
    used (language matters: French, Arabic, English lists), most
    comfortable and uncomfortable loudness levels, rollover noted.
  - Speech-in-noise result if performed.

8.3 Tympanometry and reflexes
  - Per ear: ear canal volume (ml), peak pressure (daPa), static
    compliance (ml), gradient. Jerger type auto-suggested from the
    values (A, As, Ad, B, C) with the doctor able to override. Probe tone
    (226 Hz standard, 1000 Hz for infants).
  - Acoustic reflexes ipsilateral and contralateral at 500, 1000, 2000,
    4000 Hz: present / absent / elevated, threshold.
  - Eustachian tube function test result if performed.
  - Type B with large canal volume automatically annotated "consistent
    with perforation or patent tube" as an observation, not a diagnosis.

8.4 Otoacoustic emissions and ABR
  - OAE: TEOAE or DPOAE, per ear per frequency band: present / absent /
    partial, signal-to-noise values optional. Newborn screening pass /
    refer per ear with date, and the screening stage (first, second).
  - ABR / BERA: click and tone-burst thresholds per ear, wave I, III, V
    latencies at a stated intensity, interpeak intervals I-III, III-V,
    I-V, interaural wave V latency difference, morphology comments.
    Auto-flag when the interaural wave V difference exceeds the
    configured threshold (default 0.3 ms) for the doctor to interpret.
  - ASSR thresholds if used.

8.5 Calculations (all deterministic, all on the server)
  - Pure tone average with the formula chosen by the clinic:
      3-frequency (500, 1000, 2000), 4-frequency (500, 1000, 2000, 4000,
      the WHO method), high-frequency PTA (2000, 4000, 8000), and
      Fletcher (best two of 500, 1000, 2000) available as options.
    Show which formula was used on every printout.
  - Air-bone gap per frequency and averaged.
  - Interaural difference per frequency and on PTA.
  - Hearing loss grade per ear using the WHO 2021 grades:
      normal (below 20 dB), mild (20 to under 35), moderate (35 to under
      50), moderately severe (50 to under 65), severe (65 to under 80),
      profound (80 to under 95), complete (95 and above). The clinic can
      switch to the older WHO or ASHA scales.
  - Type of loss per ear suggested from air-bone gap: conductive,
    sensorineural, mixed, with the rule shown (for example air-bone gap
    of 15 dB or more at two or more frequencies). Doctor confirms.
  - Configuration description: flat, sloping, rising, notched (with a
    4 kHz noise notch flag), cookie-bite, precipitous, corner audiogram.
  - Percentage of hearing impairment per ear and binaural using the
    AMA / AAO-HNS method (for medico-legal certificates), shown only when
    the doctor asks for it.
  - Speech-pure tone agreement check: SRT should be within about 10 dB of
    the PTA; a larger discrepancy is flagged as "check reliability".
  - Asymmetry criterion: flag when interaural difference meets the
    clinic's chosen rule (default: 15 dB or more at two adjacent
    frequencies, or 20 dB or more at one frequency, or a word recognition
    score difference of 15 percent or more). The flag text says the
    criterion was met and nothing more.
  - Significant threshold shift against the baseline audiogram, using
    the ASHA ototoxicity criteria by default (20 dB or more decrease at
    one frequency, or 10 dB or more at two adjacent frequencies, or loss
    of response at three consecutive frequencies where responses were
    previously obtained). Occupational standard threshold shift (average
    of 10 dB or more at 2, 3 and 4 kHz) available as a second rule for
    workplace medicine. The baseline audiogram is chosen by the doctor
    and marked as such.

8.6 Audiology dashboard (what the doctor sees)
  Top row: two audiograms side by side (or overlaid, toggle), PTA and
    grade per ear in large type, type of loss, tympanogram type per ear,
    word recognition per ear.
  Alerts row: asymmetry criterion met, threshold shift vs baseline,
    speech-pure tone disagreement, conductive component on an ear
    scheduled for surgery.
  History strip: every previous audiogram as a small thumbnail, click to
    overlay in a lighter color. Threshold evolution graph per frequency
    over years.
  Notes and interpretation box, with the AI assistant offering a
    descriptive draft ("Right ear: moderate sloping sensorineural hearing
    loss, PTA 42 dB HL, word recognition 88% at 75 dB HL"). The doctor
    writes the clinical conclusion.

8.7 Hearing aids and implants tracking
  - Device per ear: type (BTE, RIC, ITE, CROS, bone conduction, cochlear
    implant, middle ear implant), brand and model, serial, fitting date,
    fitted by (in-house or external audiologist), warranty end date,
    last programming date, battery type, satisfaction score, daily use
    hours if known, aided thresholds if measured.
  - Cochlear implant: side, date, surgeon, electrode array, activation
    date, mapping dates, complications, MRI conditions.
  - Candidacy checklist for hearing aids and for cochlear implantation
    (audiometric criteria as a checklist, doctor confirms).
  - Recall for annual review and warranty expiry.

8.8 Pediatric audiology specifics
  - Newborn hearing screening result and follow-up chain (screen, rescreen,
    diagnostic ABR, intervention) with dates, so the "1-3-6" targets
    (screen by 1 month, diagnosis by 3 months, intervention by 6 months)
    can be tracked.
  - Behavioral test method recorded, minimum response levels rather than
    thresholds for infants, sound-field results flagged as binaural.
  - Speech and language milestones checklist.
  - Otitis media with effusion tracking: date first seen, hearing level,
    watchful waiting start date, 3-month recall automatically proposed.

9. VESTIBULAR (VERTIGO) SUITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In vertigo, the history makes the diagnosis and the examination confirms
it. The module is built around the timing-and-triggers approach that
vestibular specialists use.

9.1 Structured vertigo history
  - Character: spinning, swaying, lightheadedness, imbalance, "about to
    faint", visual disturbance.
  - Timing: single episode / recurrent episodes / continuous. Duration of
    a typical episode (seconds, under a minute, minutes, under an hour,
    hours, days). Onset date of the first episode and of the current one.
  - Triggers: head position change (turning in bed, looking up, bending),
    standing up, loud sound or pressure (Tullio, Valsalva), visual
    motion, stress, menstruation, none (spontaneous).
  - Associated auditory symptoms: hearing loss (fluctuating or not),
    tinnitus, aural fullness, and on which side.
  - Associated neurological symptoms: diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia,
    weakness, numbness, severe headache, loss of consciousness.
  - Migraine history and features during attacks (photophobia,
    phonophobia, visual aura).
  - Nausea and vomiting, falls, injuries.
  - Cardiovascular history and medications that can cause dizziness.
  - Anxiety and avoidance behavior.
  - DHI score (25 items, 0 to 100, with physical, emotional and functional
    subscales) loaded from the questionnaire engine.
  Summary card auto-generated: "Recurrent episodes lasting seconds,
  triggered by turning in bed, no auditory symptoms, no neurological
  symptoms, DHI 48 (moderate)." This card is what the doctor reads before
  examining.

9.2 Bedside vestibular examination
  - Spontaneous nystagmus: present / absent, direction (horizontal,
    vertical, torsional, mixed), beating direction, with and without
    fixation removed (Frenzel or video goggles), first / second / third
    degree.
  - Gaze-evoked nystagmus in each direction.
  - Head impulse test: right and left, corrective saccade present or
    absent (overt / covert if video).
  - Test of skew (cover-uncover).
  - HINTS summary auto-composed from the three fields above for the
    acute vestibular syndrome case, displayed as the three findings with
    no verdict.
  - Head-shaking nystagmus.
  - Positional tests:
      Dix-Hallpike right and left: nystagmus present / absent, latency
        (s), duration (s), direction (upbeating torsional geotropic,
        downbeating, horizontal), fatigability, reversal on sitting.
      Supine roll test: nystagmus on right and left ear down, geotropic
        or apogeotropic, which side stronger.
      Side-lying test if used.
    From these, the app names the canal pattern the findings match
    (posterior canal right, horizontal canal geotropic left-stronger,
    etc.) as a "pattern consistent with", which the doctor confirms.
  - Romberg (eyes open / closed, sharpened), Fukuda stepping, tandem
    gait, Unterberger, cerebellar tests (finger-nose, heel-shin,
    dysdiadochokinesia), Dix-Hallpike contraindications checked (neck
    problems).
  - Orthostatic blood pressure when relevant.
  - Fistula test (pressure-induced nystagmus).

9.3 Vestibular laboratory tests (entry of results)
  - Video head impulse test: gain per canal (six canals), saccade
    presence, with the reference range shown.
  - Caloric test: response per ear (warm and cool, slow phase velocity),
    unilateral weakness percentage computed with the Jongkees formula,
    directional preponderance, with the clinic's significance threshold
    (default 25 percent).
  - VEMP: cervical and ocular, present / absent, amplitude asymmetry
    ratio.
  - Posturography summary if available.
  - Rotatory chair summary if available.
  - Audiogram is linked automatically (Meniere workups need it).
  - Imaging result linkage (MRI brain and internal auditory canals).

9.4 Diagnostic criteria checklists (support, not diagnosis)
  - Barany Society criteria displayed as checklists for definite and
    probable Meniere's disease, vestibular migraine, BPPV by canal,
    persistent postural-perceptual dizziness, vestibular paroxysmia,
    bilateral vestibulopathy. The app ticks the items already documented
    in the history and exam and shows which are missing. The doctor
    decides. The criteria text is stored with its version and year.

9.5 Repositioning maneuvers documentation
  - Maneuver performed: Epley, Semont, Gufoni (geotropic / apogeotropic
    variant), barbecue roll, Yacovino (anterior canal), Brandt-Daroff
    exercises prescribed.
  - Side treated, number of repetitions, nystagmus during maneuver,
    symptoms after, immediate re-test result.
  - Home exercise handout automatically added to the documents (in the
    patient's language, with pictures).
  - Recall in 1 to 2 weeks proposed.
  - Outcome recorded at the next visit (resolved, improved, persistent,
    canal conversion), feeding the doctor's outcome statistics.

9.6 Vestibular rehabilitation
  - Referral letter to a vestibular physiotherapist with the findings
    pre-filled.
  - Exercise program selection from a library (gaze stabilization,
    habituation, balance) with printable handouts.
  - DHI repeat at follow-up for outcome measurement.

10. SUB-SPECIALTY MODULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each module is a set of fields, scores and documents that attach to a
consultation when the matching complaint or diagnosis is selected. They
are not separate applications.

10.1 Tinnitus
  - Character (tonal, noise, pulsatile, clicking), side, onset,
    constant / intermittent, loudness 0 to 10, annoyance 0 to 10, sleep
    impact, concentration impact.
  - Pulsatile tinnitus red flag with synchronous-with-pulse check and
    auscultation of the neck and mastoid.
  - Tinnitus pitch and loudness matching from audiology if performed,
    minimum masking level, residual inhibition.
  - Scores: THI (25 items, 0 to 100, five severity grades), Tinnitus
    Functional Index optional, sleep and anxiety screening optional.
  - Management tracking: counseling given, sound therapy, hearing aids,
    CBT referral, medication review for tinnitus-inducing drugs.
  - THI trend graph across visits.

10.2 Voice and swallowing
  - Voice: occupation and voice demand, VHI-10 (0 to 40) or VHI-30
    (0 to 120), GRBAS by the doctor, maximum phonation time (seconds,
    normal range shown), s/z ratio, voice recording (10 to 20 seconds of
    sustained vowel and a standard sentence) attached and playable next
    to the previous one. Acoustic analysis values (fundamental frequency,
    jitter, shimmer) entered if a lab provides them.
  - Reflux: RSI (9 items, 0 to 45) and RFS from laryngoscopy, with the
    usual cut-offs shown (RSI above 13, RFS above 7).
  - Swallowing: EAT-10 (0 to 40, cut-off 3), consistencies tolerated,
    weight trend, aspiration events, pneumonia history, FEES results
    with Penetration-Aspiration Scale, videofluoroscopy report linkage,
    diet recommendation level.
  - Speech therapy referral and progress notes from the therapist (if
    given access) or scanned reports.
  - Pre- and post-treatment comparison (phonosurgery, therapy) with
    VHI and GRBAS deltas.

10.3 Sleep and snoring (adult and pediatric)
  - Adult: Epworth (0 to 24), STOP-BANG (0 to 8), neck circumference,
    BMI, blood pressure, nasal obstruction assessment, tonsil grade,
    Friedman tongue position, Mallampati, retrognathia, tongue
    scalloping, Muller maneuver on endoscopy.
  - Sleep study results entry: type (polysomnography, home sleep apnea
    test), date, total sleep time, AHI (with severity band shown: under
    5, 5 to 15, 15 to 30, over 30), RDI, ODI, lowest saturation, time
    under 90 percent, supine vs non-supine AHI, REM AHI, arousal index,
    snoring index. Upload of the full report.
  - Drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE) using the VOTE classification:
    velum, oropharynx, tongue base, epiglottis, each with degree of
    obstruction (0 none, 1 partial, 2 complete) and configuration
    (anteroposterior, lateral, concentric), sedation used, body position
    tested. Video clips attached.
  - Treatment tracking: CPAP (pressure, compliance report values,
    tolerance issues), mandibular advancement device, positional
    therapy, weight loss, surgery proposed and performed (see surgery
    module), post-treatment AHI for outcome.
  - Pediatric: OSA-18 questionnaire, snoring frequency, witnessed apneas,
    restless sleep, enuresis, daytime behavior, growth percentiles,
    adenoid size from endoscopy or X-ray, tonsil grade, with the
    adenotonsillectomy discussion checklist and information sheet.

10.4 Allergy
  - Symptom pattern: perennial / seasonal (months), indoor / outdoor,
    ARIA classification (intermittent / persistent, mild /
    moderate-severe), conjunctival symptoms, asthma coexistence, eczema,
    food allergy.
  - Skin prick test panel entry: a table of allergens (configurable
    regional panel, for example house dust mites D. pteronyssinus and
    D. farinae, grass pollens, olive pollen, cypress, Parietaria, cat,
    dog, Alternaria, Aspergillus, cockroach, storage mites) with wheal
    diameter in mm, positive histamine control and negative saline
    control values, reading time, antihistamine-free period confirmed.
    Positivity threshold shown (commonly 3 mm above negative control).
  - Specific IgE results entry per allergen with class 0 to 6 and kU/L,
    total IgE, eosinophil count.
  - Nasal provocation test result if performed.
  - Immunotherapy tracking: allergen(s), route (subcutaneous /
    sublingual), product, start date, build-up schedule with each dose
    and date and any reaction (local / systemic grade), maintenance
    phase, next dose due (recall), planned end date, symptom and
    medication score trend.
  - Environmental control handout selection based on positive allergens.
  - Allergy summary printable for the patient and for the referring
    doctor.

10.5 Head and neck (masses, thyroid, salivary, oncology)
  - Neck mass record linked to the neck map entry (6.5), with an
    ultrasound result block (size in three dimensions, echogenicity,
    vascularity, cystic component, calcification, margins) and TI-RADS
    category for thyroid nodules.
  - FNA cytology result with the Bethesda category for thyroid, and free
    structured result for other sites (benign, atypical, suspicious,
    malignant, non-diagnostic). Core biopsy and open biopsy results.
  - Oral, oropharyngeal, laryngeal, hypopharyngeal, nasopharyngeal,
    sinonasal, salivary and thyroid primary tumor records:
      site and subsite, side, size, extent by structure (from the
      relevant map), biopsy histology and grade, HPV / p16 status where
      relevant, EBV for nasopharynx, imaging findings (CT, MRI, PET-CT)
      with dates, TNM staging (clinical and pathological, with the AJCC
      edition recorded explicitly, defaulting to the 8th) and the derived
      stage group computed by the engine from the site-specific tables,
      panendoscopy findings, second primary screening.
  - Multidisciplinary tumor board sheet generated in one click: a one-
    page summary with history, exam, imaging, pathology, staging, the
    question for the board, and the board's decision recorded afterwards
    with date and attendees.
  - Treatment record: surgery (links to surgery module), radiotherapy
    (dose, fractions, dates, center), chemotherapy (regimen, cycles),
    immunotherapy, palliative care referral.
  - Surveillance schedule generated from the recall engine using the
    clinic's protocol (default follows common guideline spacing: more
    frequent visits in year 1, spacing out through year 5, then annual),
    with each visit prompting the surveillance checklist (mucosal exam,
    neck, laryngoscopy, weight, thyroid function after neck irradiation,
    dental status, swallowing, imaging due).
  - Functional outcomes: voice (VHI), swallowing (EAT-10), feeding tube
    dependence, tracheostomy status, shoulder function after neck
    dissection.
  - Salivary glands: stone (gland, size, location by ultrasound),
    recurrent sialadenitis episodes, Sjogren screening, sialendoscopy
    results, sialorrhea management.
  - Thyroid: nodule follow-up schedule by TI-RADS category and size,
    thyroid function results, pre-op vocal fold check enforced before
    thyroidectomy (an alert if missing), calcium and PTH tracking after
    surgery, post-op vocal fold check.

10.6 Pediatric ENT
  - Growth chart: weight, height, head circumference plotted on WHO
    curves, with percentile shown. Weight drives every pediatric
    prescription.
  - Recurrent acute otitis media tracking: each episode with date,
    side, treatment, so the "3 in 6 months or 4 in 12 months" type
    criteria are computed for the tube discussion.
  - Otitis media with effusion: see 8.8.
  - Adenotonsillar disease: tonsillitis episode log (feeds the Paradise
    criteria calculator), obstructive symptoms, adenoid size, growth
    impact.
  - Ventilation tube registry: see 7.3.
  - Speech and language: milestone checklist by age, hearing screening
    status, referral to speech therapy.
  - Foreign bodies: ear, nose, airway suspicion (red flag), esophageal
    (button battery is an emergency alert).
  - Congenital anomalies: preauricular pits, branchial cleft anomalies,
    thyroglossal cyst, choanal atresia, laryngomalacia, microtia,
    syndromic associations noted.
  - Newborn hearing follow-up chain: see 8.8.
  - Pediatric drug dosing calculator wired into the prescription (see
    section 12 and 14.2).
  - Parent-facing handouts in the family's language: post-
    tonsillectomy care, tube care and swimming advice, nasal saline
    technique for infants, fever and ear pain guidance.

10.7 Facial nerve
  - Onset date, side, complete / incomplete, House-Brackmann and
    Sunnybrook at each visit with a trend graph, associated ear findings
    (vesicles for Ramsay Hunt, otitis, cholesteatoma, parotid mass),
    hearing and taste changes, hyperacusis, tear production.
  - Eye protection checklist (lubricants, taping at night, ophthalmology
    referral) with a printed handout.
  - Electrodiagnostic results (ENoG percentage degeneration, EMG) with
    dates.
  - Treatment given (steroids, antivirals, with start day relative to
    onset), physiotherapy referral, surgical decompression discussion.
  - Recovery tracking and synkinesis assessment at later visits.

10.8 Epistaxis (recurrent or severe)
  - Episode log with date, side, duration, estimated volume, setting
    (home, ED), management received.
  - Risk factor checklist: anticoagulant (with INR value if warfarin),
    antiplatelet, hypertension (with today's reading), bleeding disorder,
    hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia features (family history,
    telangiectasia, epistaxis severity score), nasal steroid or oxygen
    use, cocaine use, tumor suspicion.
  - Endoscopic localization results (from 7.1) and management (7.4).
  - Hemoglobin and coagulation results entry.
  - Prevention handout and first-aid instructions for the patient.

11. QUESTIONNAIRE AND SCORING ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One engine, many instruments. The engine stores the instrument
definition (items, response options, scoring rules, subscales, cut-offs,
language versions, license notes) and every completed response as items
plus total.

11.1 Instruments shipped in version 1 (see Appendix A for scoring)
  Sinonasal: SNOT-22, NOSE
  Ear: THI, hearing handicap inventory (HHIE / HHIA) optional
  Vertigo: DHI
  Voice: VHI-10, VHI-30
  Reflux: RSI
  Swallowing: EAT-10
  Sleep: Epworth, STOP-BANG, OSA-18 (pediatric)
  Quality of life optional: a generic 5-level scale if the clinic wants it
  Clinician-rated (filled by the doctor, not the patient): Brodsky,
    Friedman, Mallampati, Lund-Kennedy, Modified Lund-Kennedy,
    Lund-Mackay, polyp grades, House-Brackmann, Sunnybrook, GRBAS, RFS,
    Penetration-Aspiration Scale, VOTE, Myer-Cotton, Sadé, Tos, TI-RADS,
    Bethesda, TNM.

11.2 How patients fill them
  - In the waiting room on a clinic tablet, launched by the assistant for
    the chosen instrument, in the patient's language, large touch
    targets, one item per screen, progress bar.
  - At home via a link or QR code sent by SMS or WhatsApp before the
    visit or before a follow-up. The link is single-use, expires, and
    shows no other patient data.
  - On paper, with the assistant entering the answers (fallback).
  - The completed score appears on the consultation cockpit with the date
    and a badge "completed by patient" or "entered by staff".

11.3 Presentation
  - Total, subscales, severity band, and the change from the previous
    administration with the minimal clinically important difference shown
    where one is published (for example around 9 points for SNOT-22).
  - Trend graph per instrument over time, with treatment events (surgery,
    therapy start) marked on the timeline.
  - Item-level view for the doctor to see which items drive the score
    (for SNOT-22, the sleep and psychological items often matter more
    than the nasal ones).

11.4 Adding instruments
  - Admin can add an instrument by defining items, options, scoring and
    translations without code, as long as it is a simple sum or weighted
    sum. Anything with complex logic is added by the development team.
  - Every instrument carries its source reference and a note on
    licensing so the clinic knows what it is allowed to use.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. CLINICAL CALCULATOR ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One place for every formula, each with inputs, validation ranges, the
formula shown to the user, the result, the reference, and a save-to-
consultation button. Formulas live only on the server.

12.1 Audiology
  PTA (all variants), air-bone gap, interaural difference, WHO grade,
  percentage of hearing impairment (monaural and binaural), significant
  threshold shift, Jongkees unilateral weakness, ABR interaural wave V
  difference.

12.2 Pediatric dosing
  Weight-based dose calculator for the ENT formulary: amoxicillin,
  amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefuroxime, cefpodoxime, azithromycin,
  clindamycin, prednisolone, paracetamol, ibuprofen, cetirizine,
  desloratadine, montelukast, oral rehydration. Inputs: weight, age,
  indication (some indications use higher doses), formulation strength
  available locally. Output: mg per dose, ml per dose for the chosen
  syrup concentration, doses per day, duration, maximum daily dose check.
  Every result shows the mg/kg/day rule used. The formulary and rules are
  editable by the clinic pharmacist or doctor because local products
  differ.

12.3 Surgical decision aids (as checklists, doctor decides)
  - Paradise criteria for tonsillectomy in recurrent tonsillitis
    (episode counts per year plus documentation features).
  - Ventilation tube criteria for recurrent AOM and for persistent OME
    with hearing loss.
  - Adenotonsillectomy for pediatric OSA: symptom, exam and sleep study
    inputs.
  - Cochlear implant candidacy audiometric checklist.
  - Septoplasty and turbinate reduction: NOSE score plus exam findings
    summary.
  - FESS: SNOT-22, Lund-Mackay, medical therapy trial documented.

12.4 Head and neck
  - TNM stage group calculator by site and edition.
  - Neck dissection level nomenclature helper.
  - Lund-Mackay CT score entry (maxillary, anterior ethmoid, posterior
    ethmoid, sphenoid, frontal 0 to 2 each; ostiomeatal complex 0 or 2;
    per side; total 0 to 24).
  - Chandler classification of orbital complications of sinusitis (I to
    V) as a structured field.

12.5 General
  BMI, body surface area, ideal body weight, creatinine clearance (for
  dose adjustment), corrected age for premature infants, expected date
  of splint or pack removal from insertion date.

12.6 Airway
  Myer-Cotton subglottic stenosis grade from percentage obstruction,
  endotracheal tube size estimate by age (for the anesthesia team and
  for pediatric airway notes).

12.7 Epistaxis and bleeding
  Epistaxis severity score for HHT, INR interpretation range display.

Every calculator result saved into a consultation is stored with the
inputs, the formula version and a timestamp, so a result can always be
reproduced later.

13. SURGERY MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ENT is a surgical specialty. The consultation frequently ends with
"we should operate." From that moment the app must carry the patient
through the surgical pathway.

13.1 Proposing surgery (from the consultation plan)
  - Procedure(s) from the ENT procedure catalog (Appendix C), side,
    urgency (elective, semi-urgent, urgent), anesthesia expected (local,
    sedation, general), setting (day case, inpatient), estimated
    duration, indication text auto-drafted from the diagnosis.
  - Generates in one click the "surgery bundle":
      procedure information sheet in the patient's language,
      consent form with the procedure-specific risks list (editable
        library, e.g. tonsillectomy: bleeding, pain, dehydration,
        infection, voice change, dental injury; FESS: bleeding, CSF leak,
        orbital injury, recurrence; tympanoplasty: graft failure, hearing
        worse, taste change, dizziness, facial nerve injury; thyroidectomy:
        recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypocalcemia, bleeding, scar),
      quote (devis) with the clinic's price list and insurance codes,
      pre-anesthesia consultation request,
      pre-operative tests request (blood count, coagulation, others by
        age and comorbidity per the clinic's protocol),
      pre-operative instructions (fasting, medications to stop such as
        anticoagulants and when, what to bring).
  - Adds the patient to the surgical waiting list.

13.2 Waiting list and pre-op checklist
  - List per surgeon with planned date, priority, days waiting, status.
  - Pre-op checklist per patient with ticks and dates: consent signed,
    anesthesia consult done, labs done and reviewed, imaging available,
    pre-op vocal fold check done (mandatory flag for thyroid and
    laryngeal surgery), hearing test done (mandatory flag for ear
    surgery), anticoagulant plan documented, pregnancy status if
    applicable, implants or pacemaker noted, blood group and antibodies
    if needed, insurance approval received, quote accepted.
  - Missing items shown in red on the operating day list.

13.3 Operating day list
  - For each patient: procedure, side (large, unmissable, matching the
    consent), allergies, anticoagulants, airway alerts, checklist status,
    surgeon, assistant, anesthetist, room, order.
  - WHO surgical safety checklist fields (sign in, time out, sign out)
    optionally recorded.

13.4 Operative note builder
  - One template per procedure family with structured fields, so a
    tonsillectomy note takes 90 seconds:
      date, surgeon, assistant, anesthetist, anesthesia type, position,
      antibiotic prophylaxis given, tranexamic acid given, steroid given,
      findings, technique (with the clinic's usual text pre-filled and
      editable), instruments or energy device, implants or grafts (type,
      lot number for traceability: tubes, ossicular prostheses, septal
      splints, packing material, nasal stents), specimens sent (site,
      laboratory, expected result date creates a recall), estimated
      blood loss, drains, packing (type, planned removal date creates a
      recall), complications (none or from list), intra-op images or
      video attached, post-op instructions, planned follow-up.
  - Procedure-specific structured fields, examples:
      Tympanoplasty: perforation size and site, ossicular chain status
        (intact, eroded incus, absent), graft material (temporalis
        fascia, cartilage, perichondrium), technique (underlay,
        overlay), approach (transcanal, endaural, postauricular),
        cholesteatoma extent if present.
      Mastoidectomy: canal wall up or down, extent of disease, facial
        nerve status, dural or sinus exposure, second look planned.
      Stapedotomy: footplate findings, prosthesis type and length.
      Septoplasty and turbinates: deviation location, technique, spur
        removed, turbinate technique (radiofrequency, submucosal
        resection, outfracture), splints and planned removal date.
      FESS: side, sinuses opened (uncinectomy, maxillary antrostomy,
        anterior and posterior ethmoidectomy, sphenoidotomy, frontal
        recess work with Draf type), navigation used, polyps removed,
        complications, packing.
      Tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy: technique (cold steel, bipolar,
        coblation), hemostasis method, adenoid size, bleeding amount.
      Microlaryngoscopy: lesion, technique (cold, laser with type and
        settings), biopsy, injection material and volume.
      Thyroidectomy: extent (lobectomy, total), nerve monitoring signal
        before and after, parathyroids identified and preserved or
        reimplanted, drain, specimen.
      Parotidectomy: extent, facial nerve branches identified and
        stimulated, immediate facial function.
      Neck dissection: levels dissected per side, structures preserved
        or sacrificed.
      Tracheostomy: indication, technique, tube type and size, sutures,
        first change date.
      Nasal fracture reduction, rhinoplasty, otoplasty, excision of neck
        lump, branchial or thyroglossal excision, salivary stone removal.
  - Every operative note feeds the surgeon's logbook (13.6) and the
    patient timeline.

13.5 Post-operative pathway
  - Default post-op plan per procedure (Appendix C), editable, creating
    appointments and recalls: for example tonsillectomy phone check at
    day 2 and visit at day 10 to 14; septoplasty splint removal at day 5
    to 7; tympanoplasty visit at 2 to 3 weeks and audiogram at 3 months;
    FESS cavity debridement at week 1 and 3 and endoscopy at 3 months;
    thyroidectomy calcium at day 1 and vocal fold check at 2 weeks.
  - Post-op visit template: pain score, bleeding, fever, oral intake,
    wound or cavity check with the relevant map, packing or splint
    removal recorded, complication capture (structured list with
    severity, useful for the surgeon's own audit), return to work or
    school date, next step.
  - Pathology result review recall with a "result explained to patient"
    tick and date.
  - Outcome measurement: the same questionnaire that justified the
    surgery is re-sent automatically at 3, 6 and 12 months (SNOT-22
    after FESS, NOSE after septoplasty, VHI after phonosurgery, THI if
    relevant, Epworth after sleep surgery), and audiograms are compared
    to the pre-op baseline for ear surgery.

13.6 Surgeon logbook
  - Every procedure with role (primary, assistant, supervising), date,
    complexity, complications, outcome. Filter by procedure and period.
  - Export for training, accreditation or personal audit.
  - Complication rate per procedure over time, private to the surgeon.

14. DOCUMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything printed comes from a template. Templates carry the clinic
header and footer, the doctor's identity and signature, and the language.
Every document is generated from structured data, previewed, and can be
edited before it is finalized. Finalized documents are stored as files
attached to the visit and cannot be silently changed.

14.1 Clinical reports
  - Consultation report (for the chart and for the referring doctor).
  - Procedure reports: nasal endoscopy, laryngoscopy / stroboscopy,
    otomicroscopy, FEES, DISE, office procedures.
  - Audiology report with the plotted audiogram, tympanograms, speech
    results, calculations and the doctor's interpretation.
  - Vestibular assessment report.
  - Allergy assessment report with the prick test table.
  - Sleep assessment summary.
  - Operative report and discharge summary.
  - Tumor board summary sheet.
  - Referral reply letter to the referring doctor ("Dear colleague, thank
    you for referring...") pre-filled with the consultation content, in
    the referring doctor's language.
  - Report writing rules: right before left, positive findings before
    negatives, negatives grouped ("no polyps, discharge or masses"),
    scores with their scale names, conclusion in the doctor's words,
    plan as numbered items. The doctor can maintain a personal phrase
    library ("my phrases") inserted with a shortcut.

14.2 Prescriptions
  - Drug search with the clinic's formulary first and the doctor's
    favorites for the current complaint at the top.
  - Structured items: drug, form, strength, dose, route (oral, nasal,
    otic, topical, injection), frequency, duration, quantity, instructions
    in the patient's language, substitution allowed or not.
  - ENT-specific prescription helpers: nasal saline irrigation protocol
    with volume and frequency, nasal steroid spray technique note, ear
    drop instillation technique with tragal pumping, taper schedules for
    oral steroids (auto-generated day by day), antibiotic duration
    defaults per indication, weight-based pediatric dosing pulled from
    the calculator with the syrup volume, "renewable" flag for chronic
    therapy (allergic rhinitis, reflux).
  - Safety checks on the prescription screen: drug allergy block,
    ototoxic drug warning when hearing loss is on the problem list,
    duplicate class warning, pregnancy and breastfeeding warning, dose
    above the maximum for weight or age.
  - Template prescriptions ("ordonnance types") the doctor saves: acute
    otitis media child, allergic rhinitis adult, post-tonsillectomy pain,
    BPPV, sudden hearing loss, acute sinusitis adult, otitis externa.
  - Printed in the clinic's legal format with the required identifiers.
  - Medication list on the patient card updated from prescriptions.

14.3 Certificates and administrative documents
  - Sick leave (work or school absence) with dates and, where required,
    diagnosis or "medical reasons" only.
  - Medical certificate (free-text with template), fitness certificates
    with ENT-specific variants: swimming with tubes, air travel after
    ear surgery, diving fitness with audiogram summary, driving fitness
    after vertigo, professional voice fitness, hearing certificate for
    employment or licensing with the percentage of impairment.
  - Certificate of surgery performed, hospitalization dates.
  - Insurance forms: configurable templates per insurer with fields
    auto-filled (diagnosis code, procedure code, dates). Kept generic
    because forms differ by country.
  - Attendance certificate.

14.4 Requests to other services
  - Imaging request: modality and protocol from an ENT list (CT sinuses
    non-contrast, CT temporal bone high resolution, CT neck with
    contrast, MRI internal auditory canals with gadolinium, MRI neck,
    PET-CT, ultrasound neck with FNA, panoramic dental X-ray, lateral
    neck X-ray for adenoids), clinical question auto-written from the
    consultation, contrast allergy and renal function flags, pregnancy
    flag, pacemaker or cochlear implant MRI warning.
  - Laboratory request: ENT panels (pre-operative, thyroid function and
    calcitonin, allergy specific IgE panel, autoimmune inner ear panel,
    infectious screen for sudden hearing loss, culture and sensitivity
    of ear discharge, HPV status, coagulation for epistaxis).
  - Audiology and vestibular test requests to an in-house or external
    lab, with the specific tests ticked.
  - Referral letters: speech therapy, vestibular physiotherapy,
    audiologist for hearing aid fitting, allergist, pulmonologist and
    sleep lab, oncology, radiotherapy, dentistry and maxillofacial,
    neurology, ophthalmology (facial palsy, orbital complications),
    pediatrics, psychology (tinnitus, PPPD). Each pre-filled with the
    relevant findings only.

14.5 Patient handouts (education)
  A library in French, Arabic and English, with pictures where they help,
  selected automatically by diagnosis or procedure and printed with the
  prescription or sent by link:
    nasal saline irrigation technique, nasal spray technique, ear drop
    technique, ear care with tubes and swimming, post-tonsillectomy diet
    and pain plan with warning signs, post-septoplasty and post-FESS
    care, post-ear-surgery care, epistaxis first aid and prevention,
    BPPV explanation and Brandt-Daroff or Epley home exercises, tinnitus
    coping and sound enrichment, hearing protection and noise, vocal
    hygiene, reflux diet and lifestyle, allergen avoidance per allergen,
    CPAP adherence tips, sleep hygiene, eye protection in facial palsy,
    voice rest after phonosurgery, tracheostomy care at home,
    thyroidectomy recovery and calcium symptoms, what to expect from a
    nasal endoscopy or laryngoscopy (pre-visit).
  Clinics can edit texts and add their own.

14.6 Output and delivery
  - Print, download as PDF, attach to the visit. Optional: send the
    patient a secure link by SMS, WhatsApp or email to download their
    documents. Optional: send the referral reply to the referring doctor
    by email.
  - Batch print of the whole visit (report, prescription, requests,
    handouts, follow-up card) in one action at the end of the visit.
  - A follow-up card for the patient: next appointment, tests to do
    before it, what to bring.

15. PATIENT TIMELINE, COMPARISON AND FOLLOW-UP ENGINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

15.1 Timeline
  - Vertical timeline of every event: consultation, procedure, test,
    document, surgery, questionnaire, phone call, external result
    received, recall sent, no-show. Each with a one-line summary and
    icons by type. Filter by type, by problem, by side.
  - Grouping by problem: click "left chronic otitis media" and see only
    the events related to that problem.
  - Hover shows the summary, click opens the full record.
  - Print the timeline as a one-page patient history summary (useful for
    referrals and for the doctor's own recall before a visit).

15.2 Comparison views
  - Audiograms: overlay of any two or more, with threshold differences
    per frequency in a table and the shift criteria evaluated.
  - Endoscopy and laryngoscopy: structure-by-structure table (previous
    vs current) with changed items highlighted, plus image pairs.
  - Scores: line graphs per instrument with treatment markers.
  - Exam maps: previous and current side by side.
  - Consultations: field-level diff of history and exam.
  - Photos: any two photos of the same region in a slider.
  - Growth: percentile curves for children.

15.3 Follow-up (recall) engine
  This is the module that stops patients from being forgotten.
  - A recall is a structured item: patient, reason, due date, window
    (e.g., plus or minus 2 weeks), what should happen (visit, test,
    phone call, questionnaire), who created it (doctor, or a rule),
    status (pending, booked, done, overdue, cancelled with reason).
  - Recalls are created three ways:
      manually from the plan ("see in 6 weeks with audiogram"),
      automatically from procedures and findings (tube inserted: check
        at 6 months; pack inserted: remove in 48 to 72 hours; splints:
        remove in 5 to 7 days; wick: remove in 2 to 3 days; specimen
        sent: review in 7 to 10 days; cholesteatoma cavity: clean every
        6 to 12 months; immunotherapy dose: next in 4 weeks),
      automatically from protocols attached to a diagnosis (head and
        neck cancer surveillance schedule; thyroid nodule follow-up by
        TI-RADS; OME watchful waiting 3 months; sudden hearing loss
        repeat audiogram at 2 to 4 weeks; post-op outcome questionnaires
        at 3, 6, 12 months; hearing aid annual review; yearly audiogram
        for ototoxic therapy or noise exposure).
    All intervals are defaults in an editable protocol table, not hard-
    coded.
  - Work list for the assistant: overdue and upcoming recalls, one-click
    to call, send an SMS or WhatsApp reminder in the patient's language,
    book, or mark unreachable (with attempt count).
  - Dashboard counters for the doctor: overdue recalls by type.
  - When the patient arrives, the cockpit shows the recall reason and
    the expected actions ("tube check: otoscopy, tympanogram").

16. IMAGES, VIDEOS AND ATTACHMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Capture from a connected endoscopy camera or webcam directly in the
    browser, or upload from a file. Short video clips (limited duration
    and size) for laryngoscopy and stroboscopy.
  - Every image is tagged with patient, date, procedure, region and side,
    and optionally attached to a specific structure on a map.
  - Annotation tools: arrow, circle, freehand, text label, measurement
    against a known scale where possible. Annotations are stored
    separately from the original so the original is never altered.
  - Side-by-side viewer for the same region across dates.
  - Selected images are placed in the report with captions
    auto-generated from the tags ("Left middle meatus, polyp grade 2,
    2026-09-18").
  - External documents: scanned CT and MRI reports, pathology reports,
    external audiograms, sleep study PDFs, previous letters, each tagged
    with type and date and, where possible, linked to the structured
    result record (a scanned audiogram can be transcribed into numbers by
    the technician; the AI assistant can propose the transcription).
  - Image consent flag for teaching use.
  - Storage lifecycle and access are covered under security (19.8).

17. DOCTOR DASHBOARD AND PRACTICE ANALYTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

17.1 Today
  - Today's list with questionnaire status and recall reasons.
  - Drafts to sign, results to review (pathology, imaging, audiology
    entered by the technician), referral letters to answer.
  - Surgical waiting list count and pre-op checklist gaps for the next
    operating day.
  - Overdue recalls by type.

17.2 My practice (private to each doctor, clinic-level for the admin)
  - Volumes: consultations, procedures, surgeries per month; new vs
    follow-up; by chief complaint; by diagnosis; by age group.
  - Referral sources and referral replies sent.
  - Outcomes: mean and median change in SNOT-22 after FESS, NOSE after
    septoplasty, VHI after phonosurgery, THI after tinnitus management,
    Epworth or AHI after sleep surgery, audiogram change after
    tympanoplasty or stapedotomy (air-bone gap closure rate to within
    10 dB or 20 dB), BPPV resolution after one maneuver, tube
    complication rates (otorrhea, blockage, early extrusion).
  - Complications per procedure over time (from operative and post-op
    notes).
  - Documentation quality: percentage of visits with a coded diagnosis,
    with a signed report within 24 hours, with a follow-up plan.
  - Time metrics: average consultation duration if the clinic wants it.
  - All charts are built from the structured data already collected.
    There is no separate data entry for analytics.

17.3 Research and export
  - Anonymized export of selected fields for a chosen diagnosis or
    procedure, with the identifiers removed and dates shifted, for
    audit or research, admin-approved and logged.
  - Cohort builder: "all patients with FESS in 2025 who completed SNOT-22
    at 6 months".

18. THE AI ASSISTANT (DELIBERATELY SMALL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything above works without AI. The assistant adds convenience at
five points, always as a suggestion the doctor accepts or rejects, and
always visibly labelled as a draft. It never writes into the record on
its own.

18.1 Free text to structured fields
  The doctor types or dictates a paragraph of history. The assistant
  proposes values for the structured fields (onset, laterality,
  duration, associated symptoms). Each proposed value is shown next to
  the field with an accept button. Nothing is saved until accepted.

18.2 Report drafting
  From the structured findings, the assistant drafts the narrative
  conclusion and the referral reply letter in the chosen language and in
  the doctor's usual style (learned from the doctor's own signed
  reports, kept private to that doctor). The template engine already
  produces the findings text; the assistant only helps with the
  conclusion and the letter prose.

18.3 Patient-friendly explanation
  Converts the diagnosis and plan into a short explanation for the
  patient in French, Arabic or English, at a chosen reading level,
  printed with the handouts. The doctor reads it before it prints.

18.4 Transcription of scanned results
  Proposes the numeric transcription of a scanned external audiogram or
  a sleep study summary into the structured fields, for the technician
  or doctor to verify field by field.

18.5 Pre-visit summary
  Before a follow-up visit, a three-line summary of the patient's ENT
  history, last plan, and what is due today, generated from the timeline
  and recalls. Useful for a doctor seeing 40 patients a day.

Explicitly out of scope: diagnosis suggestion, treatment recommendation,
image interpretation of endoscopy or CT, triage, anything that could be
read by the doctor as a clinical opinion from the software.

Every AI request and response is logged with the user, time and whether
the suggestion was accepted, so its usefulness can be measured and its
cost controlled.

19. CROSS-CUTTING FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

19.1 Languages
  - Interface in French and English at launch, Arabic later.
  - Patient-facing documents and questionnaires in French, Arabic and
    English at launch, with right-to-left layout for Arabic. Each patient
    has a document language.
  - Clinical vocabulary (findings, diagnoses, procedures) stored as codes
    with labels per language, so a report can be produced in a language
    different from the one used to enter it.

19.2 Keyboard and speed
  - Command palette (a single shortcut) to jump to any patient, module,
    template or action by typing.
  - Every map, grid and picker fully operable by keyboard. Right / left
    toggle, normal, next structure, attach image, sign, print each have
    a shortcut.
  - Audiogram grid navigation with arrow keys and typed values.
  - Dictation into any free-text field using the browser's speech input
    where available.

19.3 Autosave and drafts
  - Every field saves within seconds. A visit interrupted by a browser
    crash or a tablet battery reopens exactly where it was.
  - A visible saved indicator. Conflict handling if two users edit the
    same record (the second sees the first's changes before overwriting).

19.4 Search
  - Patients by name, phone, ID, date of birth, fuzzy and accent-
    insensitive, Arabic and Latin scripts.
  - Clinical search across a doctor's patients: by diagnosis, procedure,
    finding, score range, date range ("all patients with grade 3 polyps
    seen in 2026").

19.5 Templates the doctor owns
  - Visit templates per complaint, report phrase library, prescription
    templates, document templates, favorite diagnoses and procedures,
    default follow-up intervals. Clinic defaults plus personal overrides.

19.6 Tablet and procedure-room modes
  - A simplified touch layout for the tablet the assistant carries and
    for the procedure room screen: large targets, the current structure
    map, capture button, previous image, sign.

19.7 Audit and traceability
  - Who created, viewed, changed or printed every clinical record, when,
    from where. Viewing is logged too (break-glass access to a patient
    outside one's list requires a reason).
  - Signed records are immutable; changes are addenda.
  - Versioned templates, protocols and calculators: a report always
    knows which template version and which formula version produced it.

19.8 Security and privacy (minimum for medical data)
  - Individual accounts, strong passwords, optional second factor,
    automatic session lock after inactivity (short in shared rooms).
  - Role-based permissions as in 4.3, per clinic and per site.
  - Encryption in transit everywhere and at rest for the database and
    the file store. Images and documents stored outside the database
    with access only through authenticated, expiring links.
  - Daily backups, tested restores, retention rules aligned with local
    medical record retention law.
  - Patient data export on request and deletion or anonymization rules
    per local law.
  - No patient identifiers sent to the AI provider beyond what the
    feature needs; identifiers stripped where possible; the clinic can
    disable AI entirely.
  - Consent flag for SMS or WhatsApp contact.

19.9 Offline resilience (later phase)
  - Read-only access to today's patients and the ability to keep
    entering a visit during a short connection loss, synchronized when
    back online. Not in version 1 but the data model should not prevent it.

20. DATA PRINCIPLES AND CODING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Every finding is a coded value with side, date, source (doctor,
    technician, patient, device, AI-proposed-then-accepted) and the
    visit it belongs to.
  - Diagnoses coded with ICD-10 (the ENT chapters: H60 to H95 for the
    ear, J00 to J39 for the upper respiratory tract, C00 to C14 and C30
    to C32 and C73 for head and neck and thyroid cancer, K11 to K14 for
    salivary glands and oral cavity, R42 and H81 for vertigo, and
    others), with a display name in each language and room for a local
    code system in parallel.
  - Procedures coded with a configurable catalog mapped to the local
    nomenclature used by insurers.
  - Measurements stored with units and reference ranges.
  - Scores stored as items plus computed total plus the instrument
    version.
  - Free text always allowed but never the only place a fact lives.
  - Right and left are explicit on every paired structure. "Bilateral"
    is never stored as a single value on a paired finding; it becomes two
    findings.
  - Dates of onset are stored as either a date or a duration with a
    "recorded on" date, so "6 months" typed today still means something
    next year.
  - Reference data (allergen panels, drug formulary, procedure catalog,
    protocols, questionnaires) is versioned and editable by the clinic
    admin without code changes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
21. MVP SCOPE, PHASING AND WHAT TO LEAVE OUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The product is large. The MVP must still be small enough to ship, so the
phases below are ordered by what makes a single ENT doctor's day faster
first.

Phase 1  Foundation (weeks 1 to 6)
  Clinic and users with roles, patient registry with alerts and
  clinical background, minimal schedule and waiting list, audit, document
  templates with header and footer, print to PDF.

Phase 2  The consultation (weeks 7 to 14)
  Consultation cockpit with chief complaint templates for the ten most
  common complaints, structured history, examination with the ear, nose,
  oral cavity and neck maps, one-click normals, copy forward, diagnosis
  coding, plan items, consultation report, prescription with pediatric
  dosing and safety checks, sick leave and certificates, imaging and lab
  requests, referral letters, patient timeline, manual recalls.
  This phase alone is a usable product.

Phase 3  Office procedures (weeks 15 to 20)
  Nasal endoscopy and laryngoscopy report builders with image capture
  and annotation, Lund-Kennedy and RFS scores, otomicroscopy and ear
  procedures with tube tracking, epistaxis and other short procedure
  forms, automatic recalls from procedures.

Phase 4  Audiology (weeks 21 to 26)
  Audiogram entry and chart, speech, tympanometry, calculations, WHO
  grading, asymmetry and shift alerts, dashboard, overlay comparison,
  audiology report, hearing aid tracking, technician role.

Phase 5  Questionnaires, scores and calculators (weeks 27 to 30)
  Questionnaire engine with SNOT-22, NOSE, THI, DHI, VHI-10, RSI,
  EAT-10, Epworth, STOP-BANG; tablet and QR filling; trend graphs;
  calculator engine with the audiology, pediatric dosing and surgical
  criteria calculators.

Phase 6  Vestibular suite (weeks 31 to 34)
  Structured history, bedside exam with positional tests and canal
  pattern, maneuver documentation, criteria checklists, lab test entry.

Phase 7  Surgery module (weeks 35 to 42)
  Surgery proposal and bundle, waiting list, pre-op checklist, operating
  day list, operative note templates for the top fifteen procedures,
  post-op pathways with automatic recalls and outcome questionnaires,
  surgeon logbook.

Phase 8  Sub-specialty modules (weeks 43 to 50)
  Sleep and DISE, allergy with prick test and immunotherapy, head and
  neck with TNM and tumor board sheet, pediatric growth and episode
  logs, facial nerve, voice recordings.

Phase 9  Analytics and AI (weeks 51 onward)
  Dashboard outcomes, cohort export, the five AI assistant functions.

Not in version 1 (and probably not in version 2):
  online booking by patients, billing and accounting, pharmacy stock,
  full insurance claims processing, hospital bed management, patient
  portal beyond document download and questionnaires, telemedicine
  video, AI diagnosis or treatment suggestions, automatic image
  interpretation, integration with hospital systems (HL7 / FHIR) until a
  real customer asks, device integrations beyond simple file import.

22. THREE KILLER WORKFLOWS THE PRODUCT MUST NAIL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

22.1 Chronic rhinosinusitis, first visit to surgery, under 12 minutes
  Patient fills SNOT-22 on the tablet in the waiting room (score 58).
  Doctor selects "nasal obstruction" and "facial pressure". History
  block appears, doctor fills 8 structured fields and one free-text
  line. "Normal ear, oral cavity, neck" in one click. Nasal endoscopy
  builder: right middle meatus polyp grade 2 with purulent discharge,
  left grade 1, two photos captured and labelled. Lund-Kennedy computed.
  Diagnosis coded. Plan: nasal steroid and irrigation prescription
  template, CT sinuses request with the clinical question auto-written,
  handout on irrigation, follow-up in 6 weeks with CT and repeat
  SNOT-22 (recall created, SNOT-22 link scheduled). Batch print. At the
  follow-up, Lund-Mackay entered from the CT, FESS proposed, surgery
  bundle generated, patient on the waiting list. After surgery, the
  operative note takes two minutes, SNOT-22 is re-sent at 3, 6 and 12
  months, and the doctor's dashboard shows the improvement.

22.2 Child with recurrent ear infections and hearing concern
  Weight entered by the assistant. Screening tympanometry by the
  technician shows type B both ears. Doctor selects "recurrent ear
  infections". Episode log shows 5 episodes in 9 months (criterion
  met, displayed). Otoscopy map: bilateral dull membranes with effusion.
  Audiogram by play audiometry shows a 30 dB conductive loss. OME
  tracking started, 3-month watchful waiting recall created, parent
  handout printed in Arabic. At 3 months, effusion persists, tubes
  proposed, surgery bundle in the parents' language, tube inserted,
  tube registry starts, 6-month checks created, swimming certificate
  available on request. Two years later, the timeline shows the whole
  story on one screen.

22.3 Acute vertigo in the procedure room
  Doctor selects "vertigo". DHI filled on the tablet. Structured history
  card reads: recurrent, seconds, positional, no auditory symptoms.
  Dix-Hallpike right positive with upbeating torsional nystagmus, 5
  seconds latency, 20 seconds duration. Pattern displayed: consistent
  with right posterior canal. Epley performed, documented, immediate
  re-test negative. Handout with home exercises printed. Recall in 2
  weeks with DHI. Report signed. Total time 8 minutes, and the outcome
  feeds the doctor's maneuver success statistics.

23. BRIEF TECHNOLOGY NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The stack is decided (React / Next.js with shadcn on the front end,
FastAPI on the back end, MySQL). A few implications of this
specification, kept short:

  - All calculations, scoring and criteria evaluation run on the server,
    versioned and covered by automated tests with known-answer cases from
    the published sources. The front end only displays results.
  - Report and document generation runs on the server from templates,
    so the same structured data produces the same document every time.
  - Images and videos are stored in object storage, not in MySQL; the
    database holds metadata, tags and annotation geometry.
  - The data model is built around visits containing typed findings
    (structure, side, code, value, unit, source), not around wide tables
    with one column per possible finding. This is what makes the maps,
    the comparisons and the analytics possible.
  - Reference data (codes, questionnaires, protocols, formulary,
    procedure catalog) lives in editable tables, not in code.
  - Audit is a first-class table written by the API layer, not an
    afterthought.
  - The questionnaire links for patients need a small public endpoint
    with strong token handling and rate limiting, separate from the
    clinical API.
  - Multilingual labels are data, not hard-coded strings.
